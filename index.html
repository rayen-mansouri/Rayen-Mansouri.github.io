01 — About

Early in the discipline, deliberate about the direction

I'm a first-year Computer Science Engineering student at Esprit, in Ariana, Tunisia. My coursework covers software engineering fundamentals, but most of what I actually retain comes from taking systems apart outside of class.

I'm most interested in the practical edge of security: web exploitation for how applications fail under unexpected input, digital forensics for the discipline of reconstructing what happened from what's left behind, and binary exploitation for the low-level precision it demands.

Margin noteStill first-year. Still early. That's the point — every hour in a lab or a CTF is compounding toward something more deliberate than a hobby.

Focus Areas

02 — Three disciplines
FIG. 01

Web Exploitation

Working through how web applications actually break — injection, broken auth, logic flaws hiding in plain sight. The appeal is investigative: reading a system's assumptions, then finding the one it forgot to check.

FIG. 02

Digital Forensics

Reconstructing events from artifacts — file systems, memory dumps, network captures. Forensics rewards patience and precision over speed, which is exactly why I keep coming back to it.

FIG. 03

Binary Exploitation

Reverse engineering and exploit development at the machine-code level. It's the most unforgiving of the three, and the most honest about whether you actually understand the system.
